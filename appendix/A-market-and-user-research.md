# Appendix A. Market and user research

Research for the "Walk off the sugar spike" experiment. Done with Claude in November 2026. Prices change often; check them before you quote them.

## 1. How a consumer CGM works

1. A sensor the size of a coin sits on the back of the upper arm. A one-time applicator puts a thin flexible filament under the skin.
2. The sensor measures glucose in the fluid between cells every few minutes and sends it to the phone by Bluetooth. There are no finger pricks and no test strips.
3. A sensor works for 14–15 days. Then the user throws it away and puts on a new one.
4. In the task, a "cartridge" is this replaceable sensor. Two cartridges cover one month.
5. Accuracy is similar across the main brands. A CGM reads 5–15 minutes behind blood glucose. It can show false lows at night when the user lies on the sensor.

What the market shows:

1. The sensor is a commodity. Most players resell Stelo or Lingo at $89–99 a month. They compete on the program, not on the data.
2. Wear becomes intermittent: 2–4 sensors over 3–6 months, or "a check once a year".
3. Without the sensor, value drops. The Levels plan without a CGM kept 21% of paying users against a 30% target.
4. Many players hold retention by contract: 3–6 month prepay, exit fees, auto-renewal. This moves churn to the end of the term.
5. Growth now comes with GLP-1 drugs (Signos) and through insurers.

## 3. Key numbers

1. Nutrisense (2023): average customer life grew from 3 weeks to 7–8 months. ARPU ~$225/month, CAC ~$200, margin ~50%. A sensor costs the company $40–60 per 14 days. So month-3 retention is roughly where a customer pays back.
2. Nutrisense weight: −1.12% by month 1, −1.54% by month 2, −1.8% by month 3. About 60% of the 3-month loss happens in month 1.
3. Nutrisense: weight data exists for 7,356 users in week 1 and for 2,361 in month 3 (32%).
4. Signos (n=3,007): weight falls 1.17% a week when users log food and 0.44% a week when they don't.
5. Vively (n=7,647): users log food on 63% of days with a sensor and on 2.4% of days after they remove it.
6. Meta-analysis of RCTs: a CGM alone gives −0.7 kg, not significant.
7. Benchmarks: 57% of health and fitness app users renew after month 1 (RevenueCat). 58% of GLP-1 users stop within 12 weeks.

## 4. Why users leave: user voice

Sources: Trustpilot, App Store reviews (Signos: 4.6, ~1,000 ratings), Hacker News, press articles.

1. Price is higher than the value; the same Stelo costs $99 direct.
2. Billing traps: long plans, exit fees, charges after cancellation.
3. The sensor falls off, breaks or loses connection.
4. "I've learned it": after 2–4 weeks the user knows the patterns, and novelty is gone.
5. Users don't trust accuracy: CGM and finger-prick readings differ; false lows at night.
6. Anxiety and obsession with the chart.
7. The food log feels like homework.
8. Generic advice and weak coaching.

Why users stay: they see the body's response to food at once; a live dietitian; a visible result (weight, blood tests); a concrete new habit.

## 5. Root causes of churn by month 3 (weight-loss users)

1. New knowledge runs out. In 2–4 weeks the user learns the "bad" foods. Month 3 shows nothing new.
2. Glucose is not weight. The user came to lose weight and sees sugar. Weight plateaus by month 2–3, and the link from sugar to weight is not visible.
3. The price stays the same while value falls.
4. The habit lives in the sensor, not in the person. Logging and food choices hold only while the sensor is on.
5. Friction and failures give a reason to leave.

## 6. Do on-track users stay longer?

"On-track" means the user loses 0.5% or more of body weight a week.

1. National DPP (CDC, n=41,203): 74% of on-track users stayed to week 18 vs 41% of the others. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32616617/)
2. 4 RCTs pooled (n=289): users who lost 2% or less in month 1 dropped out about 5x more often. [Source](https://ro.uow.edu.au/eispapers/5289)
3. Innergy commercial program (n=5,274): no weight change in month 1 means 1.9x higher odds of dropping out. [Source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/osp4.304)
4. Withings (n=1,605): weight goes up in the 4 weeks before a user stops weighing. Users leave after a plateau, not after success. [Source](https://www.jmir.org/2020/3/e15790/)
5. "Success churn" exists but is small: in a tirzepatide program (n=19,693), about 10% of churned users had reached their goal. [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12786109/)

Conclusion: on-track users stay about 1.5–2x better. The data is observational, and no CGM company publishes this link.
