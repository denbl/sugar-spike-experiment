    Hypothesis

    If the app sends weight-loss users a push notification to walk as soon as glucose starts rising after a meal, more of them stay on-track with weight, and on-track users are more likely to still be with us in month 3.

    1. Push notification “walk now”
    2. Lower sugar spike
    3. Smaller dip
    4. Less hunger and snacking Assumption
    5. On-track with weight
    6. M3 retention
    • Big glucose dips after meals are linked to ~300 kcal more eaten per day. ZOE PREDICT, Wyatt 2021
    • 2–10 min of light walking after a meal lowers post-meal glucose by ~17% vs sitting. Buffey 2022, meta-analysis
    • Users who lose little in month 1 drop out far more often (~5x). 4 RCTs On-track users retain better (74% vs 41%). National DPP, n = 41,203
    • Assumption A lower sugar spike leads to less hunger for people without diabetes. Not yet proven; this test checks it.
    Who

    New weight-loss users without diabetes, enrolled in their first 14 days (first cartridge).

    Assumption The app knows the user's goal from onboarding.

    Split

    50/50 random at user level.

    Control: the same trigger fires silently and is logged, so sugar spikes can be compared between groups.

    Trigger rule
    • Glucose + mg/dL within min after a meal.
    • only; max push notifications a day.
    • “Walked” = ≥ steps within 30 min after the push notification (steps read from Apple Health / Google Fit).
    Launch with minimal engineering

    The app already sends glucose alerts. This is a new rule and new copy in the existing alert system, a 50/50 feature flag, and a HealthKit step read.

    Sample size: ~ users per group for on-track at day 28 (we enrol ); ~ per group to read M3 retention directly, so the test decides on on-track and compares M3 retention at day 90.

    Metrics
    Leading
    Share of on-track users at day 28 (decision metric) = losing ≥0.5% body weight per week (National DPP threshold). Weight from Apple Health or the in-app weight log. Assumption: weight is available
    Funnel
    Opt-in rate (allowed notifications); walk rate after sugar spikes.
    Mechanism
    Post-meal peak rise after walked vs not-walked meals.
    Guardrail
    Cartridge reorder rate at day 28 – must not drop vs control. If push notifications annoy users, they stop buying.
    Long-term
    M3 retention: the share of users who still buy cartridges in month 3. Test and control are compared at day 90.
    Decision rule

    Written before launch.

    ShipOn-track rate up ≥ pp and significant, AND reorders do not drop.
    IterateWalking is up, but on-track is flat.
    StopGuardrail breached.
